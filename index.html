About

My research addresses the epistemology and philosophy of futures studies: social epistemology and social process reliabilism, anticipatory understanding, the epistemic quality of AI-mediated foresight, and AI governance as a collective transformative experience. The guiding question is how a field that studies futures that do not yet exist can produce knowledge that is reliable, accountable and open to critique.

PhD in Futures Studies (University of Tehran); MA in Philosophy of Science (Sharif University of Technology); Erasmus guest PhD student in innovation policy, Lund University.

Journal articles

  • Two dogmas of futures ontology: the Quinean turnOpen access
    Foresight 28(6), 699–724 (2026)

    Diagnoses two assumptions shared by realist, constructivist and layered ontologies of the future, and proposes a Quinean, practice-centred meta-ontology tied to social reliabilism.

    Accepted manuscript (PDF)Published version (DOI)
  • Artificial intelligence as a collective transformative experience: a conceptual framework for rational encounterOpen access
    Foresight (2026)

    Extends transformative-experience theory from individuals to societies and turns it into a four-phase, learning-oriented model of AI governance under value change.

    Accepted manuscript (PDF)Published version (DOI)
  • The generation of scientific knowledge in futures studies and foresightOpen access
    Foresight 27(6), 1158–1178 (2025)

    Argues that futures knowledge is justified by the reliability of individual, methodological and institutional processes rather than by predictive accuracy.

    Accepted manuscript (PDF)Published version (DOI)
  • Toward a new stage for the epistemology of futures studies: Exploring social epistemologyOpen access
    Futures 153, 103237 (2023)

    Introduces social epistemology to futures studies, with attention to testimony, the scientific community and reliable processes of knowledge production.

    Accepted manuscript (PDF)Published version (DOI)
